# PMP Exam Guide

Applying, Studying, and Passing with Confidence

---

## 1. Applying for the PMP Exam

### Eligibility (you must meet **one** track)

**Option 1: With a 4-year degree**
* 36 months leading projects (within last 8 years)
* 35 hours of PM education **OR** CAPM

**Option 2: Without a 4-year degree**
* 60 months leading projects
* 35 hours of PM education **OR** CAPM

> “Leading projects” does **not** require the title “Project Manager.”

---

### Application Steps

1. Create an account with **Project Management Institute (PMI)**
2. Fill out:
   * Project descriptions (objectives, role, outcomes)
   * PM hours by process group (high level)
3. Submit and wait for approval (≈5–10 days)
4. Pay exam fee:
   * **PMI member:** ~$405
   * **Non-member:** ~$555

> Once approved, you have **1 year** to take the exam
> Applications can be **audited** (random but real)

---

## 2. Understanding the PMP Exam (This Is Critical)

### Exam Format

* **180 questions**
* **230 minutes (~3 hrs 50 min)**
* **2 scheduled 10-minute breaks**
* Online (proctored) or in-person

### Question Types
* Multiple choice
* Multiple select
* Matching
* Hotspot
* Limited fill-in-the-blank

---

### Exam Content Breakdown (PMI’s ECO)

The PMP is **NOT** PMBOK-chapter based.

**Domains:**
1. **People – 42%**
2. **Process – 50%**
3. **Business Environment – 8%**

> This is a **situational judgment exam**, not a memorization test.

---

## 3. What the PMP Is *Actually* Testing

PMI wants to know if you:

* Act as a **servant leader**
* Escalate appropriately (not too fast, not too slow)
* Favor **collaboration over authority**
* Balance **Agile and predictive**
* Protect **business value and stakeholders**

> The “correct” answer is often the *most PMI-aligned*, not the most aggressive or technically clever. *This is important*

---

## 4. Study Strategy (8–10 Week Plan)

### Week 1–2: PMP Mindset Reset

**Goal:** Stop thinking like “real life,” start thinking like PMI
* Servant leadership
* Conflict resolution
* Stakeholder engagement
* Agile values & principles

> This phase matters more than formulas.

---

### Week 3–4: Process & Flow (High Level)

**Goal:** Understand *when* things happen
* Initiating → Planning → Executing → Monitoring → Closing
* Change control
* Risk response strategies
* Quality vs scope tradeoffs

> Don’t memorize ITTOs — understand **flow and intent**

---

### Week 5–6: Agile & Hybrid (Very Heavily Tested)

**Goal:** Become fluent in Agile situations
* Scrum roles & ceremonies
* Product backlog vs sprint backlog
* Incremental delivery
* Hybrid governance

> Many failures happen here.

---

### Week 7–8: Full Practice Exams

**Goal:** Exam stamina + pattern recognition
* Take **3–5 full-length exams**
* Deep review of:
  * Why right answers are right
  * Why wrong answers are tempting
* Track weak domains

> Target: **70–75% consistently** before sitting for the actual exam.

---

## 5. How to Answer PMP Questions (The Formula)

When in doubt:
1. **Assess first** (don’t act immediately)
2. **Collaborate** (talk to stakeholders/team)
3. **Follow process** (change control, risk mgmt)
4. **Escalate last** (unless safety/legal issue)

Rarely correct:
* Fire someone
* Go straight to sponsor
* Ignore formal process
* Act unilaterally

---

## 6. What to Study vs What to Ignore

### Prioritize
✅ Agile & hybrid scenarios
✅ Stakeholder conflict questions
✅ Risk responses
✅ Change management
✅ Servant leadership language

### De-prioritize
❌ Heavy formulas (EVM is light)
❌ ITTO memorization
❌ Obscure PMBOK artifacts

---

## 7. Test Day Playbook

### Before
* Sleep > cram
* Eat protein
* Calm your nervous system

### During
* Use breaks — mentally reset
* Don’t dwell on one question
* Flag and move on

Target Pace:
* ~60 questions per 75 minutes
* Leave review buffer

---

## 8. Passing, Failing, and Retakes

### Results
* Preliminary pass/fail **immediately**
* Official confirmation within days

If you fail:
* You get **3 attempts per year**
* Domain scores guide your re-study

### Pass Rate Estimates
* Well-prepared candidates: 75–85%+
* Underprepared / mindset-misaligned: <50%
* Second attempt pass rate: Significantly higher
---

## 9. After PMP: What Changes

### Career Impact

* Strong signal for:
  * TPM
  * Program Manager
  * Delivery Lead
  * Ops / Transformation roles
* Helps in:
  * Big Tech
  * Consulting
  * Regulated industries

### Maintenance
* **60 PDUs every 3 years**
* Many PDUs come from normal work
* Other PDU credits come from reading books, doing presentations, creating resources, giving back to the profession etc.

---

## Final Truth

People fail the PMP **not** because it’s hard but because:
* They rely on real-world instincts and underprepare for the *exam*
* They underestimate Agile
* They skip mindset prep
* They don’t take enough full exams
