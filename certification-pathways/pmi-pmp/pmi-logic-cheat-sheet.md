# PMP “PMI Logic” Cheat Sheet

How to Think on the Exam

---

## 1. The PMI Default Worldview

When in doubt, assume:
* You are a **servant leader**, not a boss
* The team is **competent and motivated**
* Problems are solved through **collaboration first**
* **Process exists for a reason**. Follow it
* Business value and stakeholders matter **as much as schedule**

---

## 2. The Golden Decision Order (Memorize This)

When faced with a problem, PMI almost always wants you to:
1. **Assess / Analyze** the situation
2. **Review relevant artifacts** (plan, backlog, risk register)
3. **Collaborate** with the team or stakeholders
4. **Take action**
5. **Escalate only if necessary**

> Jumping straight to action or escalation is usually wrong.

---

## 3. Escalation Rules (Very Important)

### Escalate **ONLY IF**:
* Safety risk
* Legal/compliance issue
* Ethical violation
* Authority boundary exceeded

### Do **NOT** escalate for:
* Team conflict
* Missed tasks
* Performance issues
* Unclear requirements

PMI expects **coaching**, not tattling.

---

## 4. Change Management Logic

If a change is requested:

### Predictive / Hybrid
1. Document change request
2. Analyze impact
3. Go through **Change Control Board (CCB)**
4. Update baselines
5. Communicate

### Agile
* Discuss with Product Owner
* Reprioritize backlog
* No CCB

> Never implement a change “just this once.”

---

## 5. Agile vs Predictive — How PMI Chooses

### Agile is preferred when:
* Requirements change
* Stakeholders want early value
* Innovation or uncertainty exists

### Predictive is preferred when:
* Requirements are stable
* Regulatory constraints exist
* Cost/scope are tightly fixed

Hybrid is often the **correct answer**.

---

## 6. Conflict Resolution Hierarchy

From best → worst (PMI logic):
1. **Collaborate / Problem-solve**
2. Compromise
3. Smooth
4. Force
5. Withdraw

PMI loves **collaboration** almost to a fault.

---

## 7. Stakeholder Logic

When stakeholders are unhappy:
* Engage early
* Listen first
* Clarify expectations
* Adjust communication approach

Do not:
* Ignore them
* Blame them
* Surprise them later

---

## 8. Risk vs Issue (Trick Area)

* **Risk** = May happen → plan response
* **Issue** = Has happened → take action

Risk responses:
* Avoid
* Mitigate
* Transfer
* Accept

Issues ≠ risk register updates only.

---

## 9. Quality vs Scope vs Schedule

PMI priority order:
1. **Quality**
2. Business value
3. Schedule
4. Scope

> Cutting quality to hit a deadline is almost always wrong.

---

## 10. Team Performance Problems

If a team member underperforms:
* Coach first
* Understand root cause
* Provide support/training
* Use formal HR last

> “Replace them” is almost never correct.

---

## 11. Documentation Logic

PMI loves:
* Plans
* Registers
* Logs
* Backlogs

But:
* **Agile values working solutions over documentation**
* Don’t over-document in Agile scenarios

---

## 12. Procurement & Vendors

If vendor issues arise:
* Refer to the contract
* Use formal procurement process
* Collaborate before penalizing

> Don’t renegotiate informally.

---

## 13. Ethics (Zero Tolerance)

If you see:
* Bribery
* Data falsification
* Conflict of interest

→ Stop work and escalate immediately.

No discussion. No compromise.

---

## 14. Exam Red Flags (Usually Wrong Answers)

Avoid answers that say:
* “Immediately”
* “Force”
* “Ignore”
* “Escalate to sponsor”
* “Fire”
* “Work overtime without approval”

PMI hates heroics.

---

## 15. The One-Sentence Rule

If you can’t decide, choose the answer that:

> **“Protects stakeholders, follows process, and empowers the team.”**

---

## Ultra-Short Exam Mantra

Read this before you click *Start Exam*:

> *Assess → Collaborate → Follow process → Deliver value → Escalate last*

---

# Most Importantly
The exam is a test of your ability to pass the exam *using PMI's knowledge.* If you have project management experience, some of this knowledge will feel "wrong" or ineffective based on your lived expeience. *Taking the exam is about passing the exam.* That's it.
